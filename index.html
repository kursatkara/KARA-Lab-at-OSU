We combine high-fidelity simulation (DNS and LES) with physics-guided reduced-order and machine-learning models to predict the unsteady flows that govern flight safety: the dynamic stability of reentry capsules, ship airwakes, urban atmospheric turbulence, and hypersonic boundary-layer transition.

At a glance

Kursat Kara directs the Kara Aerodynamics Research Laboratory (KARA Lab) at Oklahoma State University. The lab works on data-driven aerodynamics and reduced-order modeling, entry-capsule dynamic stability, ship airwakes and urban winds for drones and advanced air mobility, hypersonic boundary-layer transition, flow control, and quantum computing for fluid dynamics. The lab's tools are high-fidelity simulation (DNS, LES, high-order methods) combined with scientific machine learning. The work is funded by NASA, the U.S. Navy (NAWCAD), NSF, the FAA, and IBM Quantum.

He earned his Ph.D. at Old Dominion University, supported by NASA Langley, with a dissertation on hypersonic boundary-layer receptivity. He worked as a CFD engineer at New England Analytics on projects for Sikorsky Aircraft, was a postdoctoral scholar at Penn State on large-eddy simulation of hot supersonic jets, and was a founding faculty member of the first aerospace engineering program in the UAE at Khalifa University before joining OSU in 2019. He was elected an AIAA Associate Fellow in 2026. He serves on the editorial board of Scientific Reports and guest-edits its Quantum Computing collection.

Dynamic stability of reentry capsules

Physics-Guided Multifidelity Learning for Characterization of Blunt-Body Dynamic Stability (80NSSC23K0231, with a paid extension)

Blunt reentry capsules can become dynamically unstable in pitch at supersonic and transonic speeds, where small-amplitude oscillations grow while large-amplitude oscillations decay to a limit cycle. The lab characterizes this behavior from free-flight CFD of the Genesis capsule, using multifidelity data fusion, Bayesian estimation of pitch-damping derivatives, neural-operator surrogates, dynamic mode decomposition, and equation discovery.

Setup
Genesis sample return capsule; free-flight (free-to-oscillate) CFD at M∞ = 1.44, α0 = 2° and 20°.
Team
Ashraf Kassem and Shafi Al-Salman Romeo (Ph.D.); Kaden Thompson, Rachel Thibodeau, and Zachary Lohmar (undergraduate); alumnus Furkan Oz. Collaborator: Omer San.

Ship airwakes and real-time wake inference

Artificial Intelligence for Real-time Wake Inference in Ship Environments (AIRWISE), N00421-26-1-0004

Helicopter and drone operations at sea are limited by the turbulent airwake shed by a ship’s superstructure. AIRWISE runs GPU-accelerated LES of the NATO Generic Destroyer, validated against wind-tunnel measurements, and trains AI surrogates that infer the airwake in real time from sparse sensor data.

Setup
NATO Generic Destroyer; GPU-accelerated LES validated against 1:50-scale wind-over-deck velocities (NRC) and 1:200-scale surface pressure at several yaw angles.
Team
Mashrur Shejan (Ph.D.), Sabbir Hossain (M.Sc.), Gabe Kindt (undergraduate). Collaborator: Ryan Paul.
Papers
Validation and airwake results presented at NAWCAD quarterly reviews, 2026.

Turbulence loading on AAM VTOL vehicles

A94: Effect of Turbulence on Aircraft Structural Loading of AAM VTOL Vehicles (25-C-UAS-OKSU-01)

Advanced air mobility (AAM) VTOL aircraft will take off and land among high-rise buildings, inside the most turbulent part of the urban atmospheric boundary layer. This ASSURE project creates a validated turbulent wind environment, an ABL inflow with building-induced wakes and gusts resolved by LES, for flight-dynamics simulation of AAM VTOL vehicles and the structural loads they experience.

Wind-aware navigation for drones in urban airspace

NRI: INT: Safe Wind-Aware Navigation for Collaborative Autonomous Aircraft in Low Altitude Airspace (1925147, with an REU supplement)

Small drones in cities fly through turbulent wind fields that mesoscale weather models do not resolve. The lab simulated the urban atmospheric boundary layer with LES, compressed it into POD–LSTM and convolutional-autoencoder reduced-order models, and coupled those winds to drone and AAM flight dynamics to support wind-aware navigation.

Quantum computing for fluid dynamics

Exploring Quantum Computing Paradigms for Fluid Dynamics ($70,000 in IBM Quantum credits)

Quantum algorithms may speed up the solution of the partial differential equations behind CFD. The lab developed a quantum PDE solver for the viscous Burgers’ equation and a Chebyshev-point formulation that improves its efficiency, and now builds hybrid classical–quantum lattice Boltzmann methods that run on IBM quantum hardware rather than classical emulators.

Papers

Hypersonic boundary-layer transition

Direct Numerical Simulation of Broadband Acoustic Metasurfaces to Stabilize Acoustic Modes in Hypersonic Boundary Layer (Research Initiation Grant); Hypersonic Boundary-Layer Receptivity to Solid Particulates

Where the boundary layer turns turbulent sets the heating and drag on a hypersonic vehicle. DNS of Mach 6 flow over a blunt cone tracks how free-stream acoustic disturbances enter the boundary layer as first- and second-mode (Mack-mode) waves. Left alone, the waves grow, the flow transitions, and the surface heat flux rises; a localized cooling strip and an acoustic metasurface stabilize the waves and delay or eliminate transition, keeping the flow laminar.

Team
Alumnus Furkan Oz (Ph.D. 2024); undergraduates Thomas Goebel, Jared Greif, Evan Evans, and Zackary Mitchell. Code on GitHub.
Papers
Oz et al., JSR 2023, Oz & Kara, Sci. Rep. 2024, Kara et al., AIAA J. 2011

Flow-separation control with sweeping jet actuators

Sweeping Jet Actuator: An Innovative Approach for Separated Flow Control (Khalifa University Internal Research Fund); Design of Fluidic Oscillators for Active Flow Control (Khalifa University–KAIST Seed Project)

Sweeping jet actuators are fluidic oscillators with no moving parts: feedback channels make the exit jet sweep side to side. Without actuation, the flow over a wing separates; with the sweeping jets on, it stays attached even beyond the stall angle of attack. The lab studies how internal geometry and mass flow rate set the oscillation frequency and models the actuator with DMD-based and causal state-space reduced-order models.
